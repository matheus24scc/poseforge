POSEFORGE design direction — ideation board
Rethinking the workspace · Jul 2026

Reforge the workspace.

Today the app is three panels of buttons fighting for attention around a viewport. This board proposes one clear spatial idea — Process on the left, a pure interactive Stage in the center, References on the right — plus a reworked pose editor, an iterative pipeline that keeps every version, and a calmer visual system that steps away from orange.

01 — Why it feels off

The panels don't tell you what they're for.

Everything is a button

Source, Extract, Sheet, Mesh, Rig, Generate, Refine — a wall of amber buttons at the same weight. Nothing signals what to do next.

Mixed responsibilities

The right rail does bone controls and generation and jobs. Inputs, controls and outputs are all tangled together.

The stage is passive

Posing happens through a bone dropdown and three sliders in a side panel — never by touching the character you're looking at.

02 — The spatial model

One idea, three zones.

Every pixel earns a role. The left tells you where you are. The center is what you're working on — nothing but the visual, fully interactive. The right holds what you're working with.

LEFT
Process

What you're doing. The build pipeline as a living spine — steps, status, versions.

CENTER
Stage

Only the visual. Pose by dragging the character directly — no prompts, no buttons in the frame.

RIGHT
References

What you're working with. Inputs, pose & face libraries, prompts — the things you feed the stage.

inputs flow left → right into the stage  ·  results flow back out to references

03 — The visual system

Cool graphite, one electric signal.

Amber reads a little "Anthropic". We move to a neutral cool-graphite base — which also lets the mid-gray 3D stage sit naturally in the frame — with a single high-voltage azure signal reserved for the one action that matters right now, and a warm vermilion only for live selection in the viewport.

Signal discipline. Only one azure element is on screen at a time — the primary next action. Everything else is neutral. That single rule is what fixes the "wall of buttons" and rebuilds hierarchy.

05 — The pose editor, fixed

Grab the joint. Refine with numbers.

The dropdown-plus-three-sliders flow survives as a refinement tool — but posing starts by clicking a joint on the character and dragging its gizmo. Coarse by hand, exact by number.

The selected bone shows a rotation gizmo on the model. Same bone syncs the numeric panel — two views of one truth.

Numbers for precision

Once it "clicks," the XYZ readout is great for exact, repeatable angles and mirroring left↔right limbs.

Symmetry & snap

Mirror a pose across the body, snap to 15° increments — small helpers that make hand-posing feel professional.

06 — Iterative by design

Every step keeps its versions.

Generation is a slot machine — you re-roll. So each object in the pipeline holds a stack of assets, not a single result. Pick a source, re-mesh from it, compare two rigs, promote the winner. Nothing is destroyed.

The active version is what flows downstream. Change it and the steps below know they're stale — a subtle "re-run to update" cue appears, never an auto-wipe. This is the "store multiple assets per object" model made visible.

07 — Jobs, made ambient

Work happens in the background, quietly.

Meshy and Veo take minutes. Jobs shouldn't own a whole panel — they collapse into a status pill in the top bar, expandable into a tray. Progress also shows in place, right on the pipeline step it belongs to.

Survives reload. A job with a provider task ID resumes polling on reopen — the pill just reappears mid-progress.

2

Never blocks. You keep posing and browsing libraries while a mesh cooks. Results land as a new version on the right object.

3

Cost up front. Each job carries its estimate; the tray totals the session so BYO-key spend is never a surprise.

08 — Light mode

Same bones, daylight.

The graphite softens to a warm cream-gray — nothing is stark white, so it's easy on the eyes for long sessions. The stage lightens too, to a soft neutral studio gray (kept neutral so the captured render stays clean). Azure holds as the signal.
